I wrote "The Width of One Hand," a standalone Strawberry Creek Voices interlude that can be read two ways. Three readers can pass the page between Surface Water, Underflow, and Deep Silence. A solo reader can follow any one voice through its three passages and get a complete miniature story. Surface Water begins with an alder leaf caught at a culvert mouth. Underflow follows a thread of water through a joint in the concrete. Deep Silence follows a child who grows up knowing how to give directions to the crossing long before anyone asks her which way the creek runs. The final scene imagines a future opening above the creek. I left the piece unnumbered so it can sit anywhere in the buried-creek sequence. It is ready to publish as plain text. [the-width-of-one-hand.txt] STRAWBERRY CREEK VOICES THE WIDTH OF ONE HAND A reading for three voices Read the numbered passages in order, passing the page at each change of voice. If you are reading alone, try a second reading with just one voice. Each has its own way through the same crossing. 1. Surface Water An alder leaf comes down sideways. Its stem catches on the lip of the culvert. For a moment the whole leaf shivers there, bright enough to see the little holes where something ate. 2. Underflow Before the crossing was this wide, a plank went from one bank to the other. In rain the underside took up water. Long after the top was dry, you could lay a hand underneath and bring it back wet. 3. Deep Silence A child puts a hand against the new wall. On the far side, close enough for the thickness of that hand to matter, water runs. 4. Surface Water Stem first now. A flash of yellow turns edgewise and goes in. Overhead a bicycle ticks the length of the crossing. Down here the leaf takes longer. 5. Underflow Fine sand has gathered along a joint in the floor. A thread of clear water enters at the joint, even in dry weather. It cuts a groove a fingertip could follow all the way to the main current. 6. Deep Silence Years later the child can give directions to this corner. Past the bakery, left at the light. Someone asks which way the creek runs. For the first time in giving those directions she turns and looks downhill. Let the room be quiet for one breath. The last three passages imagine a future opening. 7. Surface Water Through a gap where a slab has been lifted, rain hits moving water. One drop makes a ring. One hits the same place a little later. The first ring is halfway out of the light. 8. Underflow The saw cut ends above the old joint. Sand still lies along it. For an hour around noon you can see each grain throw its small shadow, and the groove the clear thread keeps open. 9. Deep Silence At the rail she shows her son the groove. He follows it with one finger in the air. When he reaches the place where the two waters meet, his finger keeps going. She moves her hand along the rail to give his elbow room. READING NOTES Theme How a person learns where water goes. Voice layers Surface Water, Underflow, Deep Silence. Elements Alder leaf, plank, culvert joint, sand, rail. Moral note The smallest act of making room happens at the rail. Placement Standalone interlude for the buried-creek sequence.
